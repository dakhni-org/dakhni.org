Glossary of Dakhni Terms

Dakhni, Urdu and Persian words used across this archive — alphabetically arranged.

aap
Language · Urdu/Dakhni

The formal second-person pronoun — "you" in the respectful register. A hallmark of Dakhni tehzeeb; contrast with the brusque tu. The slow, formal gait of Hyderabadi conversation depends heavily on the consistent use of aap.

Related terms tehzeeb, zaban, miyan

B

Back to A–Z ↑
biryani
Food · Persian/Urdu

The layered rice dish that is the most emblematic preparation of Hyderabadi cuisine. The Hyderabadi kachchi biryani — raw marinated meat layered with parcooked rice, sealed under a dome of dough and slow-cooked — is distinguished from all northern variants by the kachchi technique and the local spice combination.

Related terms dum pukht, haleem

Explore Hyderabadi biryani, Deccan cuisine

Back to A–Z ↑
char-bagh
Architecture & craft · Persian

A four-quadrant formal garden, divided by water channels into four sections — the classical Persianate garden form. The Chowmahalla Palace at Hyderabad is organized around a char-bagh courtyard.

Word origin Persian chār (“four”) + bāgh (“garden”), describing the four-part garden plan.

Related terms rauza, zari

Explore Architecture

D

Back to A–Z ↑
dargah
Sufi practice · Persian

The shrine and tomb of a Sufi saint. A dargah is at once a spiritual centre, a free kitchen (langar), and often a place of arbitration. The annual urs at a dargah draws pilgrims of every faith.

Word origin From Persian dar (“door”) and gāh (“place”); the word also carries the sense of a threshold or court.

Related terms urs, langar, rauza, sajjada-nashin

Explore Sufism

dhikr
Sufi practice · Arabic

Remembrance of God; the central Sufi practice of repeated invocation of the Divine names or phrases. Can be silent (khafi) or vocal (jali); the form varies by silsila. The Naqshbandis are known for silent dhikr; the Qadiris for vocal dhikr.

Word origin Arabic dhikr means “remembrance” or “mention.”

Related terms Tasawwuf, silsila, Ihsan

Explore Sufism

divan
Literature & music · Persian

A collected body of poetry arranged alphabetically by the last letter of the rhyme scheme. Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah left a substantial Dakhni divan in Dakhni — the first reigning sovereign in South Asia to compose a vernacular literary divan.

Related terms zaban, marsiya

Explore Music

dum pukht
Food · Persian/Urdu

"Slow-cooked under steam" — the technique of sealing a vessel with dough and cooking over a low flame, allowing the food to cook in its own vapours. The basis of Hyderabadi biryani and haleem.

Word origin From Persian dam (“breath” or “steam”) and pukht (“cooked”).

Related terms biryani, haleem

Explore Deccan cuisine

G

Back to A–Z ↑
gharana
Literature & music · Hindi/Urdu

A school or lineage of classical music or dance, defined by a shared master, style, and repertoire. The Hyderabadi gharana of Hindustani music developed under Asaf Jahi patronage.

Related terms qawwali, divan

Explore Music

H

Back to A–Z ↑
haleem
Food · Arabic

A slow-cooked preparation of pounded wheat, lentils and meat — a dish with origins in Arab and Persian cooking, transplanted to Hyderabad and given its distinctively spiced Dakhni form. It is closely associated with Muharram and Ramazan.

Related terms biryani, dum pukht, Muharram

Explore Hyderabadi haleem, Deccan cuisine

hau
Language · Dakhni

"Yes" in the Dakhni dialect — one of the most distinctive markers of Dakhni speech that sets it apart from northern Urdu. Heard in old Hyderabad, Aurangabad and Bidar.

Related terms kaiku, nakko, zaban

Explore Dakhni language

Back to A–Z ↑
Ihsan
Sufi practice · Arabic

The third dimension of Islam in the Hadith of Jibreel: "to worship Allah as though you see Him; and if you see Him not, then know that He sees you." The science that addresses this dimension is Tasawwuf (Sufism).

Related terms Tasawwuf, dhikr

Explore Sufism

ijaza
Sufi practice · Arabic

A formal license of teaching, granted by a scholar to a student attesting that the student has mastered a particular text or discipline and is qualified to transmit it. Sufi silsilas are sustained by documented chains of ijazas.

Word origin Arabic ijāza means “permission” or “authorization.”

Related terms silsila, khalifa, madrasa

Explore Sufism

imambara
Architecture & craft · Urdu/Persian

A hall built for the commemoration of Imam Hussain, used for Muharram majlis (gatherings). The Bara Imambara at Hyderabad pre-dates its more famous Awadh namesake at Lucknow.

Related terms Muharram, marsiya

Explore Architecture

K

Back to A–Z ↑
kaiku
Language · Dakhni

"Why" in the Dakhni dialect — one of a cluster of Dakhni-specific words (kaiku, nakko, hau, miyan) that mark the dialect apart from the Urdu of the north.

Related terms hau, nakko, miyan

Explore Dakhni language

khalifa
Sufi practice · Arabic

In the Sufi context, an authorised successor (or one of several successors) appointed by a shaykh to transmit the silsila and initiate disciples in the shaykh's place. Distinguished from a biological heir.

Related terms silsila, ijaza, sajjada-nashin

Explore Sufism

khanqah
Sufi practice · Persian

A Sufi hospice or lodge — the building in which a shaykh and his disciples live, worship, and receive visitors. The khanqah at Khuldabad, established by Burhanuddin Gharib c. 1327, was the first Sufi institution of the Deccan.

Word origin Persian khānqāh denotes a Sufi lodge or residence.

Related terms dargah, dhikr, langar

Explore Sufism

L

Back to A–Z ↑
langar
Sufi practice · Persian/Punjabi

The free communal kitchen maintained at a dargah or gurdwara, open to all regardless of faith, caste or status. A defining feature of both the Sufi and the Sikh sacred traditions in the Deccan.

Related terms dargah, khanqah, urs

Explore Sufism

M

Back to A–Z ↑
madrasa
History & learning · Arabic

An Islamic school — formally, a place of teaching. The Mahmud Gawan Madrasa at Bidar (1472), with its three-storey Iranian façade and library of three thousand manuscripts, was the foremost madrasa of the fifteenth-century Deccan.

Word origin Arabic madrasa derives from the root d-r-s, associated with studying.

Related terms ijaza, zaban

Explore Mahmud Gawan Madrasa, Deccan heritage

marsiya
Literature & music · Arabic/Urdu

An elegiac poem in memory of Imam Hussain, recited during the Muharram commemorations. The Dakhni marsiya tradition, rooted in the Bahmani period (14th c.), is one of the oldest continuous literary traditions of the Deccan.

Word origin From Arabic marthiya, an elegy or lament.

Related terms Muharram, imambara, divan

Explore Music

miyan
Language · Persian/Dakhni

An affectionate term of address in Dakhni — used for a man, a child, or anyone one speaks to fondly. One of the words that most immediately marks a Dakhni speaker.

Related terms aap, tehzeeb, kaiku

Explore Dakhni language

muhalla
Language · Arabic/Urdu

A neighbourhood or quarter of a city — especially the tightly knit residential neighbourhoods of old Hyderabad and Bijapur, each typically centred on a mosque or shrine.

Word origin Arabic maḥalla refers to a locality or stopping place.

Related terms miyan, zaban, dargah

Explore Dakhni language

Muharram
History & learning · Arabic

The first month of the Islamic Hijri calendar; the occasion of the most important Shia commemorations for the martyrdom of Imam Hussain at Karbala (61 AH / 680 CE). In the Deccan, Muharram processions and marsiya recitations have continued since the Bahmani period.

Related terms marsiya, imambara, haleem

Explore Deccan heritage

nakko
Language · Dakhni

"No" or "don't" in the Dakhni dialect — the distinctive Dakhni negation. "Nakko karo" ("don't do it") is instantly recognisable as Hyderabadi speech.

Related terms hau, kaiku, zaban

Explore Dakhni language

Q

Back to A–Z ↑
qawwali
Literature & music · Arabic/Urdu

Sufi devotional music performed at dargahs, characterized by call-and-response singing, hand-clapping and the building of ecstatic intensity. Associated especially with the Chishti order; Thursday nights at the dargah of Bandanawaz at Gulbarga and Yousufain Sharif at Hyderabad are the principal qawwali occasions of the Deccan.

Word origin Related to Arabic qawl (“utterance” or “saying”), via the South Asian devotional tradition.

Related terms dargah, urs, dhikr, gharana

Explore Qawwali, Music

R

Back to A–Z ↑
rauza
Architecture & craft · Arabic/Persian

A tomb or mausoleum, specifically one associated with a saint or sovereign. The rauza of Burhanuddin Gharib at Khuldabad (d. 1337) is the oldest Sufi rauza of the Deccan.

Word origin From Arabic rawḍa, “garden”; also used for a tomb or shrine.

Related terms dargah, urs, char-bagh

Explore Architecture

S

Back to A–Z ↑
sajjada-nashin
Sufi practice · Persian/Urdu

Literally "one who sits on the prayer-mat" — the hereditary custodian of a dargah, responsible for its administration, the langar, and the urs calendar. The sajjada-nashin lineages at Gulbarga and Khuldabad are continuous from the saints themselves.

Word origin Persian sajjāda (“prayer mat”) + nashīn (“one who sits”).

Related terms dargah, khalifa, urs

Explore Sufism

silsila
Sufi practice · Arabic

"Chain" — the documented line of Sufi teachers tracing back through the Companions to the Prophet ﷺ. A precise scholarly claim, comparable to a hadith isnad. The Deccan's four classical silsilas are Chishti, Qadiri, Suhrawardi and Naqshbandi.

Word origin Arabic silsila literally means “chain” or “sequence.”

Related terms khalifa, ijaza, dhikr

Explore Sufi lineages, Sufism

T

Back to A–Z ↑
Tasawwuf
Sufi practice · Arabic

Sufism — the science of Ihsan within traditional Sunni Islam. Not a sect but one of the three classical sciences of the religion (alongside 'Aqidah and Fiqh). The great expositors of Tasawwuf — Imam al-Ghazali, Imam al-Qushayri — all wrote from within the four Sunni schools of law.

Related terms Ihsan, dhikr, silsila

Explore Sufism

tehzeeb
Language · Arabic/Urdu

Cultivation, refinement, courtesy — the Hyderabadi word for the ethos of polite speech and gracious behaviour that the Asaf Jahi court cultivated and that still marks old Hyderabadi culture. The formal aap, the half-remembered couplet quoted at the close of an argument, the slow gait of conversation.

Word origin From Arabic tahdhīb, “refinement” or “cultivation.”

Related terms aap, miyan, zaban

Explore Dakhni language

U

Back to A–Z ↑
urs
Sufi practice · Arabic/Persian/Urdu

The annual death-anniversary gathering at a saint's shrine — literally "wedding", referring to the saint's union with the Divine. The urs calendar of the Deccan runs near-continuously: Bandanawaz at Gulbarga in Dhu'l-Qa'da, Yousufain at Hyderabad on the 11th of Rabi al-Thani, and dozens of smaller urs through the year.

Word origin Arabic ʿurs means “wedding”; Sufi usage evokes union with the Divine.

Related terms dargah, sajjada-nashin, qawwali

Explore Sufism

Z

Back to A–Z ↑
zaban
Language · Persian

Language, tongue. Zaban-i-dakhni — "the language of the Deccan" — is the earliest name for what became Dakhni/Urdu as it took shape in the bazaars and courts of the Bahmani Sultanate.

Word origin Persian zabān means both “tongue” and “language.”

Related terms divan, aap, hau

Explore Dakhni language

zari
Architecture & craft · Persian

Gold (or silver) thread used in weaving and embroidery. Zari tapestry borders define the Paithani sari; zari brocade is the basis of Himroo fabric woven at Aurangabad.

Word origin Persian zar means “gold”; zarī denotes gold thread or gold-work.

Related terms char-bagh, divan
