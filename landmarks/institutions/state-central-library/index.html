Institutions of the Deccan

State Central Library (Asafia)

Hyderabad · Established 1891 · Afzal Gunj since 1936

Home›Landmarks of the Deccan›Institutions of the Deccan›State Central Library (Asafia)

Established1891[1]
Initial coreThe personal library of Moulvi Syed Hussain Bilgrami[1]
Present buildingFoundation 1932 · Library moved in 1936[1]
CityHyderabad
The LibraryFounding◆The Building◆The Collections

The State Central Library traces its institutional beginning to 1891, when scholar and administrator Moulvi Syed Hussain Bilgrami (Nawab Imad-ul-Mulk Bahadur) helped establish a library whose initial core came from his own collection. Later known as the Asafia Library, it moved in 1936 to the purpose-built Afzal Gunj premises on the Musi. The Government of India's India Cine Hub describes it today as the apex of Telangana's library system, housing about 500,000 books and magazines together with rare palm-leaf manuscripts.[1]

I · FoundingImad-ul-Mulk · 1891

The beginning

From Syed Hussain Bilgrami's collection to the Asafia Library

The library began in 1891 through the efforts of Moulvi Syed Hussain Bilgrami, whose personal library formed the institution's initial core.[1] It was subsequently known as the Asafia Library in honour of the Asaf Jahi dynasty. Rekhta's institutional profile likewise identifies Bilgrami's collection as the starting point of the library.[2]

II · The BuildingAfzal Gunj · 1932–1936

The riverside building

Foundation laid under Mir Osman Ali Khan; occupied in 1936

The library's present home belongs to a later phase of its history. The foundation stone at Afzal Gunj was laid in January 1932 by Mir Osman Ali Khan, the seventh Nizam, and the Asafia Library shifted into the completed building in 1936 during his silver-jubilee period.[1] The building's palace-like halls, high ceilings and riverside position made it one of the most prominent civic structures on the Musi.

III · The CollectionsBooks & Manuscripts

The collections

A major public collection serving Telangana

The State Central Library remains a public reference institution and the apex of the state's library system. Government of India material records a collection of about 500,000 books and magazines, including rare palm-leaf manuscripts; Rekhta also notes holdings in Urdu, Persian, Arabic, English, Telugu and Hindi.[1,2]
