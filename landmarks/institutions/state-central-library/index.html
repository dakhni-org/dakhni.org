State Central Library (Asafia)

Hyderabad · Established 1891 · Afzal Gunj since 1936

Home›Landmarks of the Deccan›Institutions of the Deccan›State Central Library (Asafia)

Established1891[1]
Historic nameAsafia Library[2]
Present buildingAfzal Gunj · occupied 1936[2]
Public roleState Central Library of Telangana[1]
Collection scaleAbout 500,000 books and magazines reported by Government of India[2]
The Asafia Library1891◆Afzal Gunj◆Public Library◆Manuscript Legacy

The State Central Library is one of Hyderabad’s major surviving public institutions from the Asaf Jahi period. Government sources place its establishment in 1891 and identify its earlier name as the Asafia Library. The institution later moved to the large Afzal Gunj building on the Musi, where it became the apex public library of the state.[1,2]

I · FoundationHyderabad · 1891

From private collection to public institution

The library began during the reign of the sixth Nizam

Government accounts date the library to 1891. India Cine Hub attributes its establishment to the efforts of scholar-administrator Moulvi Syed Hussain Bilgrami, also known as Nawab Imad-ul-Mulk Bahadur, and states that his personal books formed an important part of the initial collection.[2]

The Hyderabad district government describes the State Central Library as the largest public library in the state and places it among the institutions that shaped the city’s literary development.[1]

II · The BuildingAfzal Gunj · 1932–1936

A civic landmark on the Musi

The purpose-built library belongs to the later Asaf Jahi modernization of Hyderabad

The present Afzal Gunj building represents a later phase of the institution. India Cine Hub records that its foundation stone was laid in January 1932 under Mir Osman Ali Khan and that the Asafia Library moved into the completed building in 1936.[2]

Its scale—large halls, high ceilings and a prominent riverside position—gave the library a civic presence different from a private manuscript collection. The move turned the institution into a visible part of Hyderabad’s twentieth-century public architecture.

III · The LibraryBooks, Periodicals, Public Access

A collection built for public use

The institution’s importance lies in access as well as preservation

Government of India material reports roughly half a million books and magazines in the State Central Library and describes it as the apex of the state library system. The collection includes material in several languages associated with Hyderabad’s multilingual intellectual life.[2]

That public role distinguishes the State Central Library from specialist archives. It is not merely a heritage building or manuscript repository; its historical importance also comes from the idea that a major princely-state collection could become a public reading institution serving the city and, later, the state.

IV · The Manuscript LegacyAsafia Manuscripts

A history shared with Telangana’s manuscript institutions

The Asafia name links several later preservation histories

The Telangana Government Oriental Manuscripts Library and Research Institute traces its own institutional origins to the 1891 library and records the growth of a large manuscript collection in Urdu, Arabic, Persian and other languages before later administrative reorganizations created a dedicated manuscripts institution.[3]

This means the Asafia legacy should not be described as though every manuscript associated with the old library remains physically in one modern institution. The more accurate picture is a shared institutional history: the public State Central Library and the specialized manuscripts institute both inherit parts of Hyderabad’s longer project of collecting, preserving and making knowledge available.[3]
