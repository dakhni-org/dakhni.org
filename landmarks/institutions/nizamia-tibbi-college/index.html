Institutions of the Deccan

Nizamia Tibbi College

Hyderabad · Reorganised as a college 1938

The Nizamia Tibbi College gave the older Unani medical tradition of the Deccan a modern institutional form in stages: a Hyderabad hakim tradition traced to around 1810, a teaching hospital endowed in 1925 under the seventh Nizam, Mir Osman Ali Khan, and a formal reorganisation into a college in 1938. Unani — "the Ionian" — was the Greco-Arabic system of humoral medicine developed in the Islamic world from the works of Hippocrates and Galen, brought into India by the Delhi Sultans and patronised throughout the Deccan since the Bahmani period. The Nizamia Tibbi College gathered the lineages of the great Deccan hakim families into a single teaching institution with a fixed syllabus, examinations and a teaching hospital, and remains one of the principal Unani medical schools of South Asia.[1]

I · The Tradition Hakims of the Court

The tradition

The Unani lineages of the Asafi Deccan

Unani medicine in the Deccan ran in family lineages of hakims attached to the Asafi court, a tradition in Hyderabad traced back to around 1810 — the most distinguished being the Hakim Ajmal Khan circle of Delhi, with which the Hyderabad establishment kept close ties. The seventh Nizam in particular took a personal interest in Unani and, in 1925, endowed the institution with an extensive teaching hospital.[1]

II · The School Qanun & Beyond

The school

Classical Unani texts joined to modern medical science

Formally reorganised as a college in 1938, its syllabus combines the classical Unani texts — Ibn Sina's Qanun, the Sharh al-Asbab of Nafis, the works of Razi and others — with modern anatomy, physiology and pharmacology. Students sit four-year and postgraduate examinations, train in the attached hospital, and qualify as registered Unani physicians.[1]

III · Today Government Nizamia

Continuing role

A principal teaching institution of the Unani system

Now the Government Nizamia Tibbi College, it functions under the central Indian framework for traditional medicine and remains a principal teaching institution of the Unani system.[2] Its library and pharmacy preserve some of the most important Unani materials in India.
