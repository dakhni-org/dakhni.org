Cuisine of the Deccan

Biryani

Hyderabad · Kachchi Dum

Home›Heritage of the Deccan›Cuisine›Biryani

Signature ofHyderabad[1]
TechniqueKachchi dum — raw-marinated, sealed and slow-cooked[1]
OriginPersian court technique met Telugu spice under the Asaf Jahi kitchen[2]

Biryani is the signature dish of Hyderabadi cuisine, born where the Persian khansamas of the Asaf Jahi court met the Telugu cooks of the Deccan plateau. Hyderabad's own style is kachchi dum: meat marinated raw, layered with rice that has been parboiled separately, sealed under a dough lid, and cooked over slow embers until the steam alone has done the work.[1,2]

I · The TechniqueKachchi Dum

Meat, rice, and patience

Nothing about it is hurried

The meat is marinated raw in yoghurt, ginger, fried onions and spice, then layered with parboiled rice and sealed under a dough lid to cook over slow embers. Done well, the meat is melting, the rice is dry-grained, and every spoonful carries saffron at one bite and chilli at the next.[1,2]

II · Kachchi vs. PakkiA Distinction Hyderabad Insists On

Raw marinade, not pre-cooked meat

The difference between a Hyderabadi feast and any other biryani

A Dakhni feast is judged by the kachchi biryani, in which raw meat is layered with rice and cooked together in one sealed pot. Anything made instead with meat cooked separately before layering is pakki — and Hyderabad will tell you the difference matters.
