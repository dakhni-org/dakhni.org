The Living Heritage of the Deccan

Heritage of the Deccan

Seven pillars of a six-century civilisation

Home›Heritage of the Deccan

The Deccan plateau holds a culture six centuries in the making — a meeting place of Persian court and Telugu village, of Sufi shrine and Maratha plain. What survives today as Dakhni heritage is not one tradition but seven, woven together over the lifetimes of eighteen sultans and seven Nizams.[1,2]

I · Zubaan

Language & Poetry

A tongue forged in the bazaar, refined at the court

Dakhni — also written Dakhini, Dakani or Deccani — developed as a southern form of Hindustani/Urdu in the medieval Deccan. From the Sufi literary traditions of Gulbarga to the poets of the Qutb Shahi and Adil Shahi courts, it built a substantial literature before northern Urdu became a dominant literary standard.[3]

Cuisine

Persian saffron, Telugu chilli, Marathi grain, Mughal patience

Hyderabadi cuisine was born when the Persian khansamas of the Asaf Jahi court met the Telugu cooks of the deccan plateau — a kitchen of kachchi biryani, haleem, marag, mirchi ka salan and khubani ka meetha, tied together by a glass of Irani chai.

Music & Performing Arts

From Ibrahim's veena to a qawwali at the dargah

Ibrahim Adil Shah II called himself Jagat Guru and made music central to the Kitab-i-Nauras. Qawwali at Deccan dargahs, Hindustani court traditions and the marsiya of Muharram remain important strands of the region's musical inheritance.

Explore → IV · Imarat

Architecture & Monuments

Bahmani fortresses, Qutb Shahi minarets, Adil Shahi domes

A third great Indo-Islamic tradition, distinct from Delhi and the Mughals. The Charminar, Golconda, the Qutb Shahi tombs, and at Bijapur the Gol Gumbaz — a 44-metre dome whose whisper carries seven echoes.

Explore → V · Hunar

Crafts & Material Culture

Black metal, woven gold, painted line

Bidriware — the silver-inlaid blackened alloy craft associated above all with Bidar — remains a defining Deccan art, alongside Paithani silk, Himroo brocade, pearl work and Deccan miniature painting.

Explore → VI · Tasawwuf

Sufi Tradition

The path the court followed

Before the sultans, the Sufis came south — Burhan-ud-Din Gharib to Khuldabad, Bandanawaz to Gulbarga. They taught in the local tongue, fed travellers, mediated between Hindu and Muslim peasants, and helped make Dakhni a literary language.

Explore → VII · Tehzeeb

Festivals & Living Culture

A year in the Dakhni calendar

From Ramazan iftar at Charminar to Bonalu in the old city, from Muharram marsiya to Ugadi pacchadi — a plural calendar bound together by tehzeeb, the courtesy of speech and habit left by the Asaf Jahi durbar.
